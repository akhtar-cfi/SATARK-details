## Body

{First name},

I'm Gajendra — I co-founded [Cars24](https://www.cars24.com/), one of India's largest auto-tech companies. Earlier this year you applied to join Crashfree India. We didn't take that forward, and I'm writing to you personally because a different seat has opened, one that may fit you better. You raised your hand for this mission once without anyone asking you to. That matters to me, because I'm looking for nation-builders: people who are genuinely made to serve.

Here's what I'm building, and why.

You already know why Crashfree India exists. Every year, India records around five lakh road crashes and loses 1.7 lakh lives, the silent equivalent of a Boeing crashing every single day, leaving behind families pushed into poverty, children pulled out of school, and communities carrying sudden, irreversible loss. This devastation isn't accidental, it's systemic.

One of those systems is enforcement, and it's where we've already proven something. It started as a billboard: in Bengaluru, with the city traffic police, we put up India's first AI billboard at Trinity Circle. Cameras read a number plate, check it against government records, and flash pending challans, lapsed insurance and expired PUC on a screen in about ten seconds. It went viral. ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/bengaluru/big-boss-watching-digital-billboard-at-bengalurus-trinity-circle-displays-traffic-violations-in-real-time-3741955) · [Media4Growth](https://www.media4growth.com/showcase/campaigns/bengalurus-ai-billboard-by-cars24-puts-traffic-violations-on-the-big-screen-79885))

That billboard became SATARK, the same engine, now a live tool with traffic police that tells an officer which vehicle to stop, and why, in real time. It runs in Jaipur, Bengaluru and Gurugram: 5.4 lakh+ vehicles screened, ₹53 crore+ in unpaid dues surfaced, 600+ interceptions. The billboard was the ad. SATARK is the product.

Now the reason for this email. I want to take SATARK to the 100 districts where India loses the most lives, by the end of 2027, and I need one person to build and run that vertical like it's their own startup. Not business development, not a policy desk, not a support seat: this one is yours to own. You'd sit across police commissioners, transport secretaries and ministries, convince them, sign them, and stay until officers are actually stopping vehicles. You'd have a small team of one or two strong people, and Cars24's engineering, installation and funding behind you. But nobody hands you a plan on Monday: the map of India is yours to draw.

I won't pretend it's glamorous. It's slow rooms, cold follow-ups, months where nothing moves, and then a district goes live and vehicles get stopped that would have killed someone. That trade is the whole job.

If this pulls at you, two things:

1. Your latest CV.
2. A 4–5 minute video (a link is fine). Pick a problem you genuinely care about (not necessarily road safety), anything this country looks away from. Tell me your solution, and convince me it matters. A few examples:
   - Why is a footpath no city's KPI, when walking is how most Indians reach a bus stop?
   - Why is a child riding pillion without a helmet still a normal sight?
   - Why do crash victims die in the golden hour with a trauma centre twenty minutes away?
   - Why do government school toilets get built and never maintained?

   Those are mine. Yours can be anything, roads or far from them. Back it with data. Anticipate my questions. We are not testing polish. We are testing conviction.

Two dates, because we move fast and I want people who do too:
- Reply to acknowledge by **29 September, end of day**. Even one line.
- Send the video by **1 October, end of day**.

The day-to-day and the full picture are in the attached document. Every reply comes to me.

Warm regards,
Gajendra Jangid
